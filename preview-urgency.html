Finding the late deal

Every treatment of the overdue row failed for the same reason, so none of these four is a treatment of the overdue row — it is identical in all of them. What changes is what is around it, what the card itself does, or where the card sits. The problem was never that the red line was too quiet; it is that nothing else on the card is.

Spend colour only on exceptions

Nothing is added. The ordinary card gives colour up: the call key outlines instead of filling, the documents bar greys unless a document is actually rejected, the stage capsule goes neutral and the amount drops back to black. The overdue row is unchanged from today — and it is now the only coloured thing in the column.

B

The card is the signal

Not an element on the card — the card. A deal with something overdue inverts: dark ground, white name, the band and the task row the only colour on it. At board scale you are not looking for a red line inside a card, you are looking for the dark card, which is a much easier thing to find. Costs nothing in height.

C

Stop making them look

The cheapest answer: late deals rise to the top of their column under a heading that counts them, and everything else sits below. The marker can stay as quiet as it is today, because nobody has to find it — position does the work, and position is free.
